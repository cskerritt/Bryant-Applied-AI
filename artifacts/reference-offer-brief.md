# Reference Offer Brief: Applied Revenue Lab

## Administrative information

- **Project name:** Applied AI Sales Funnel Lab
- **Company:** Applied Revenue Lab, a fictional teaching company
- **Offer:** AI Lead Response Sprint
- **Version:** 0.2
- **Date:** August 12, 2026
- **Status:** Revised after Module 2 evidence review; recommended only for a low-risk educational prototype, pending human Gate 2 retrace and signoff

## 1. Provisional buyer segment

The provisional research cohort is a U.S. employer firm classified in NAICS 54, Professional, Scientific, and Technical Services, with 10–99 employees at the enterprise level. The firm is hypothesized to receive recurring B2B inbound inquiries and to lack a consistently inspectable process for capture, qualification, assignment, follow-up, handoff, or measurement.

The 2022 Statistics of U.S. Businesses table contains 102,728 employer firms in the selected industry and size bands [E-001]. That count establishes a measurable recruiting population only. It does not establish a serviceable market, problem prevalence, readiness, demand, or willingness to pay.

Firms with 20–99 employees are a higher-readiness hypothesis, not a proven better segment. All-industry Census data show higher reported AI use in the 20–49 and 50–99 bands than in the 10–19 band, but AI use is not evidence of lead-process complexity or buying intent [E-005].

The owner, revenue leader, sales-operations leader, or named process owner may be the visitor, user, influencer, or economic buyer. The primary buyer remains unresolved.

### Operational qualifiers to test

The first research and prototype cohort should require:

- A primarily B2B revenue model
- Recurring inbound inquiries
- At least two people touching an inquiry before opportunity or final disposition
- An existing system of record
- Permission to review de-identified workflow evidence without customer content
- A named person who owns or can change the process

These are screening hypotheses, not validated purchase predictors.

## 2. Triggering condition

The proposed trigger is an observable process event, such as an unclear or duplicated handoff, unreconciled inquiry and opportunity counts, a CRM change, a team change, or leaders being unable to explain who owns the next action and final disposition.

No event has been shown to create urgency or purchase behavior in the provisional segment. Trigger and urgency remain hypotheses.

## 3. Current consequences

Potential consequences may include delayed actions, duplicated work, ambiguous ownership, incomplete dispositions, or uncertain reporting. Peer-reviewed B2B research makes the underlying qualification, handoff, and information-process mechanisms plausible [E-006; E-007; E-008]. Their prevalence, severity, economic consequence, and causal effect in the provisional cohort are not established.

## 4. Existing alternatives

The buyer might:

- Keep the current manual process
- Assign an employee to monitor a shared inbox
- Change CRM configuration
- Hire a sales-operations consultant
- Adopt a lead-routing or automation product
- Increase lead generation without changing response operations

Which alternatives buyers actually use, why they choose them, and when outside help is preferred remain unresolved.

## 5. Offer statement

Applied Revenue Lab proposes a fictional fixed-scope consulting sprint that maps how inquiries are captured, qualified, assigned, followed up, handed off, and measured, then documents a future-state workflow and implementation priorities.

This is the safe offer promise. It describes work products rather than proven results. Any AI assistance is bounded by the approved use case, data restrictions, source verification, and a named human reviewer. NIST AI 600-1 informs those controls but does not certify the offer or eliminate risk [E-010].

## 6. Proposed deliverables

- Current-state lead-response map
- Lead ownership and qualification matrix
- Future-state response workflow
- Draft response and follow-up playbook
- Measurement and implementation plan

The evidence makes these process components plausible [E-007; E-008; E-012]. It does not establish that buyers prefer the package, regard each deliverable as useful, or will pay for a fixed-scope sprint.

## 7. Exclusions and boundaries

- No promise of a particular revenue, conversion, or response-time result
- No universal response-time service level or five-minute rule
- No replacement of the buyer’s CRM or sales staff
- No legal, privacy, cybersecurity, or regulatory assurance
- No automated sending or production-system access during a fit review
- No use of sensitive or confidential customer data in the educational demonstration
- No NIST certification, FTC approval, customer proof, or commercial-validation claim

## 8. Primary conversion

Request a 20-minute fit review.

This is a bounded prototype hypothesis, not a validated buyer preference. The request language must identify the educational context and must not imply confirmed availability, acceptance as a client, urgency, or creation of a contractual relationship.

## 9. Secondary conversion

Request the Lead Response Checklist. For the educational deployment, this will be demonstrated only with synthetic submissions unless a separate, approved data-handling process is established.

## 10. Likely objections

| Objection | Current status | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “We already have a CRM.” | Research distinguishes technology from customer-information processes, but target-segment behavior is unknown [E-008] | Interviews and workflow evidence about capture, integration, access, and use |
| “Our lead volume is too small.” | Unresolved; no minimum volume is supported | Evidence about frequency and the conditions under which a diagnostic is feasible and valuable |
| “AI creates privacy or quality risk.” | Material; NIST guidance supplies controls, not assurance [E-010] | A use-case data map, prohibited inputs, verification, failure handling, and human approval |
| “Our staff can fix this internally.” | Plausible alternative | Buyer interviews about capacity, skills, and implementation barriers |
| “A sprint will not change behavior.” | Material risk | Evidence about implementation, adoption, and accountability requirements |
| “Why pay for diagnosis rather than implementation?” | Unresolved | Artifact walkthroughs, alternative comparisons, and a human-approved offer test |

## 11. Claims boundary

Without additional evidence, the public site must not claim:

- A five-minute response requirement, 21-times qualification effect, or 100-times contact effect
- A universal response-time SLA
- That 70% of leads are ignored, including attribution of that claim to Sabnis et al.
- A percentage increase in revenue or conversion
- A guaranteed reduction in response time
- A particular return on investment
- Experience serving named clients or industries
- Proprietary technology or proven AI models
- Regulatory compliance, security certification, or risk elimination
- Testimonials, case studies, or customer counts
- Proven urgency, willingness to pay, price acceptance, or market demand

The Federal Trade Commission requires an appropriate reasonable basis for objective express and implied claims before dissemination [E-011]. The claim ledger controls both wording and the page’s overall net impression.

## 12. Highest-priority research questions

1. Does the 20–99 employee subgroup produce more qualifying and purchase-readiness evidence than the 10–19 subgroup?
2. Which role experiences the problem, owns the process, and authorizes outside help?
3. What recent observable event creates action rather than hypothetical interest?
4. Which breakdowns recur in de-identified workflow evidence?
5. Which deliverables enable a named user to make or implement a decision?
6. Will a qualified buyer make a real financial commitment, and at what scope and price?
7. Is a 20-minute call the appropriate initial commitment?
