# Reference Offer Brief: Applied Revenue Lab

## Administrative information

- **Project name:** Applied AI Sales Funnel Lab
- **Company:** Applied Revenue Lab, a fictional teaching company
- **Offer:** AI Lead Response Sprint
- **Version:** 0.1
- **Date:** August 12, 2026
- **Status:** Initial teaching hypothesis

## 1. Initial buyer segment

The initial audience is hypothesized to be an owner, revenue leader, or sales-operations leader at a small B2B professional-services firm that receives inbound inquiries but lacks a consistent process for responding, qualifying, assigning, and following up.

The exact firm-size and lead-volume thresholds are intentionally unresolved. They require evidence rather than a convenient invented number.

## 2. Triggering condition

The proposed trigger is a visible process breakdown, such as inconsistent response ownership, leads remaining without a documented disposition, or leaders being unable to describe what happens between inquiry and scheduled sales conversation.

This is a hypothesis to research, not a claim about the market.

## 3. Current consequences

Potential consequences may include delayed responses, duplicated work, uncertain pipeline reporting, or viable inquiries receiving no follow-up. The frequency, magnitude, and economic effect are not established.

## 4. Existing alternatives

The buyer might:

- Keep the current manual process
- Assign an employee to monitor a shared inbox
- Change CRM configuration
- Hire a sales-operations consultant
- Adopt a lead-routing or automation product
- Increase lead generation without changing response operations

Module 2 must examine which alternatives are actually used and why.

## 5. Offer statement

Applied Revenue Lab proposes a fictional fixed-scope consulting sprint that maps the existing lead-response process, identifies control gaps, defines a future workflow, and produces an implementation and measurement plan. Any AI-assisted element remains subject to human review and the client’s systems, data, and policies.

## 6. Proposed deliverables

- Current-state lead-response map
- Lead ownership and qualification matrix
- Future-state response workflow
- Draft response and follow-up playbook
- Measurement and implementation plan

The reference website will describe these as proposed offer elements, not as a proven methodology.

## 7. Exclusions and boundaries

- No promise of a particular revenue, conversion, or response-time result
- No replacement of the buyer’s CRM or sales staff
- No legal, privacy, cybersecurity, or regulatory assurance
- No automated sending or production-system access during a diagnostic call
- No use of sensitive or confidential customer data in the educational demonstration

## 8. Primary conversion

Request a 20-minute diagnostic call.

The request language must not falsely imply confirmed availability, acceptance as a client, or creation of a contractual relationship.

## 9. Secondary conversion

Request the Lead Response Checklist. For the educational deployment, this will be demonstrated only with synthetic submissions unless a separate, approved data-handling process is established.

## 10. Likely objections

| Objection | Current status | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “We already have a CRM.” | Reasonable anticipated objection, not researched | Interviews on the difference between tool ownership and workflow consistency |
| “Our lead volume is too small.” | Unknown | Evidence about the conditions under which process redesign is worthwhile |
| “AI creates privacy or quality risk.” | Material and plausible | Specific use cases, data flows, controls, and applicable policies |
| “Our staff can fix this internally.” | Plausible alternative | Buyer interviews about capacity, skills, and implementation barriers |
| “A sprint will not change behavior.” | Material risk | Evidence about implementation, adoption, and accountability requirements |

## 11. Claims boundary

Without additional evidence, the public site must not claim:

- A percentage increase in revenue or conversion
- A guaranteed reduction in response time
- A particular return on investment
- Experience serving named clients or industries
- Proprietary technology or proven AI models
- Regulatory compliance, security certification, or risk elimination
- Testimonials, case studies, or customer counts

## 12. Highest-priority research questions

1. Which role owns inbound-lead response in the most plausible target firms?
2. What observable events cause those firms to examine the process?
3. Which breakdowns are frequent enough to justify an external engagement?
4. Which deliverables would a buyer regard as usable rather than merely advisory?
5. What level of commitment is appropriate for the first website conversion?
