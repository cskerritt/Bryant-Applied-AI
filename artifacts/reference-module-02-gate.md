## What did not pass

The following are still hypotheses and must not be presented as established facts:

- the best specialization within NAICS 54;
- whether 20–99 employees predicts greater readiness than 10–19;
- the economic buyer or primary website visitor;
- prevalence, frequency, severity, and economic consequence of the problem;
- the event that creates urgency;
- willingness to pay;
- price;
- preference for a fixed-scope sprint;
- the value of individual deliverables;
- the best call to action; and
- commercial demand for an AI-branded service.

## Proposed safe offer promise

The educational prototype may say:

> The proposed sprint maps how inquiries are captured, qualified, assigned, followed up, handed off, and measured, then documents a future-state workflow and implementation priorities.

This is a description of work products, not a claim of business results.

## Prohibited claims

The prototype must not publish or imply:

- a five-minute response rule;
- 21-times qualification or 100-times contact claims;
- a universal response-time SLA;
- that 70% of leads are ignored, including attribution of that figure to Sabnis et al.;
- any guaranteed or typical revenue, pipeline, conversion, response-time, or ROI outcome;
- proven demand, market leadership, customers, testimonials, case studies, or proprietary technology;
- NIST certification, FTC approval, legal compliance, security assurance, or risk elimination; or
- false urgency, limited availability, or confirmed acceptance of a submitted request.

The 2011 HBR timing study may remain in the internal evidence log only. It may justify measuring client timing but may not supply a public performance multiplier.
